Artificial intelligence (AI) is the field of creating computational systems that perceive environments, learn, reason, solve problems, communicate, and make decisions to achieve goals. Its applications include search engines, chatbots, virtual assistants, autonomous vehicles, game-playing systems, medical tools, robotics, and generative systems that create text, images, audio, video, and code.

AI research encompasses machine learning, neural networks and deep learning, natural language processing, computer vision, robotics, planning, knowledge representation, logic, probability, optimization, and decision theory. Modern progress has been driven by large datasets, powerful GPUs, deep learning, and transformer-based models such as large language models. Research also pursues artificial general intelligence (AGI), though current systems remain limited, error-prone, and prone to hallucinations.

AI has advanced science, drug discovery, medical diagnosis, finance, entertainment, logistics, education, military operations, and disaster management. However, it raises major concerns involving privacy, copyright, bias and discrimination, misinformation and deepfakes, labor displacement, autonomous weapons, explainability, concentration of power, and emotional or social harms. Its substantial energy, water, mineral, and infrastructure demands also create environmental impacts.

Because of these opportunities and risks, researchers and governments are developing AI-safety techniques, ethical frameworks, transparency tools, testing standards, and regulations. International efforts include national AI strategies, global safety summits, the EU AI Act, and treaties addressing human rights and democratic governance. Fundamental debates remain over machine consciousness, AI rights, alignment with human values, and the possibility of future superintelligence or existential risk.
